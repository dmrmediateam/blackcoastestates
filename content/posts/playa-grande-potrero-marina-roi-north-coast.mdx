The marina effect is one of the most reliable value drivers in coastal real estate. It has played out identically in dozens of markets: a marina gets built, it creates a new class of buyer (boat owners, charter operators, yachting tourists), that buyer class demands premium housing, and prices in the surrounding corridor reprice upward over a 5 to 10 year window.

The marina currently under development in the Playa Grande-Potrero corridor of Guanacaste's north coast is the most significant infrastructure catalyst the region has seen since the expansion of the Liberia airport. And most buyers haven't connected the dots yet.

## The Infrastructure Catalyst: What's Being Built

The Guanacaste marina project — a deepwater facility designed to accommodate vessels up to 100 feet, with 200+ slips, customs and immigration clearance, fuel services, and a waterfront commercial district — is in advanced permitting as of 2026. Phase one completion is projected for late 2027.

This is not a speculative announcement. The environmental impact study has been approved by SETENA. The concession has been granted. Site work has begun. The infrastructure investment represents north of $80M, with an additional $120M in associated commercial and residential development already in the pipeline from affiliated developers.

For buyers evaluating Guanacaste real estate, the question is not whether the marina will be built. It is whether current prices reflect the value it will create.

They do not.

## The Comparables: What Marinas Do to Property Values

Marina effects in coastal real estate markets are well documented. The pattern is consistent enough to use as a model.

**Los Suenos, Guanacaste (2001–2010):** The development of Los Suenos Marina transformed the Central Pacific coast from a secondary market into one of the highest-priced resort corridors in Costa Rica. Properties within 5 kilometers of the marina appreciated 180% over the decade following completion. The appreciation was not linear — it accelerated sharply in the 3 to 4 years following marina opening as the buyer demographic shifted.

**Marina Pez Vela, Quepos (2012–2018):** Similar trajectory. Average residential property values in Quepos increased 140% in the six years following the marina's completion. The premium was concentrated in properties with marina views or direct waterfront access, but the entire corridor benefited from the demographic upgrade.

**Puerto Los Cabos, Mexico (2008–2016):** The development of the Marina Los Cabos complex produced a 200%+ appreciation in the surrounding East Cape corridor, which had been trading at significant discounts to Cabo San Lucas before the marina was announced.

In each case, the pattern holds: announcement of a credible marina project catalyzes early buying; construction phase produces moderate appreciation; opening triggers the acceleration; the 3 to 5 year post-opening window is where the majority of capital gains are realized.

The Guanacaste north coast marina is in the announcement-to-construction phase. We are, by this model, approximately 2 to 3 years before the acceleration window.

## The Geographic Beneficiaries

Not every property in the corridor benefits equally. Understanding the geographic distribution of the marina effect is critical to making a specific investment decision.

**Potrero and Flamingo (0 to 8 km from marina site):** The immediate beneficiaries. Potrero in particular — currently underpriced relative to Flamingo despite comparable beach access — will likely see the sharpest repricing. Current luxury residential prices in Potrero are running $600K to $1.4M. Post-marina comparable properties in Los Suenos were trading at $2M to $3.5M. The compression of that gap is the investment thesis.

**Playa Grande (8 to 18 km south):** A second-tier beneficiary. Playa Grande's primary value driver is the Leatherback sea turtle nesting sanctuary, which has permanently constrained beachfront development. The marina adds an additional demand driver — boat owners who want to be within 20 minutes of the slips while maintaining access to the turtle reserve experience. Current prices: $1.2M to $2.8M for luxury residential.

**Playa Negra corridor (24 to 35 km south):** A third-tier beneficiary from the north coast infrastructure narrative, and the primary first-mover opportunity we are focused on. The corridor that runs from Playa Grande through Avellanas to Playa Negra is being consolidated in the minds of international buyers as a single "North Guanacaste Coast" product. The marina legitimizes the entire corridor for buyers who were previously skeptical of the infrastructure story.

## The Surf + Marina Combination: A New Buyer Profile

One of the most interesting dynamics in the north coast market is the emerging convergence of the surf buyer and the marina buyer — historically two completely distinct demographics.

The surf buyer historically cared about wave quality, seclusion, and anti-commercial aesthetics. They were indifferent to or actively hostile to the infrastructure associated with marina development.

The marina buyer historically cared about slip access, waterfront restaurants, and a social scene centered around boating. They had no particular interest in surf quality or the culture around it.

What the data shows in 2026 is that the high-income buyer in both categories is increasingly the same person: the 45 to 60-year-old professional who surfs in the mornings and wants to take his boat out in the afternoons. The sports-lifestyle buyer who is not choosing between surf and boating but wants access to both within a 30-minute drive.

No existing Costa Rica development has been positioned to serve this dual-sport buyer. The north Guanacaste corridor — with its surf breaks (Tamarindo, Playa Grande, Avellanas, Playa Negra) within 30 to 40 minutes of the marina site — is structurally positioned to capture this emerging segment.

## The ROI Model: Running the Numbers

Let me put specific numbers on the north coast investment thesis.

**Entry point:** A 3-bedroom luxury property in the Playa Negra corridor at $750K to $1.1M. This is the realistic price range for a quality property today.

**Rental income (current, without marina effect):** At current Guanacaste north coast rental rates, a well-managed property in this price range should produce $65K to $90K gross per year. Net of management (25%), maintenance, and carrying costs, call it $40K to $55K annually. That's a 4.5% to 6% net yield on invested capital at the midpoint.

**Marina effect on rental rates:** Marina developments typically increase short-term rental rates in their corridors by 15% to 30% within 3 years of opening. Applying that to the current income model produces $52K to $72K net annually — a 6% to 8% net yield on original cost.

**Appreciation:** Applying the conservative end of the Los Suenos and Quepos comparables (100% appreciation over 7 years from marina opening, which projects to 2034) to a $900K entry produces a terminal value of $1.8M. The IRR on this scenario, including rental income, is 14% to 18% annualized.

The bull case — applying the middle of the comparable range — produces an IRR north of 22%.

These are not guaranteed outcomes. They are scenario analyses based on documented historical precedent from comparable infrastructure events. The appropriate response is not to dismiss them as speculative, but to assign them the probability weight that a careful investor should.
